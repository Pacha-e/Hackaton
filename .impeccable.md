## Design Context

### Users

**Citizens** — Medellín residents of all demographics and tech literacy. They arrive with a problem (a complaint, a request, a report) and need to feel that the institution received them and will respond. Context: any device, often on mobile, occasionally stressed. Their job: file a request in under 3 minutes and get a confirmation they trust.

**Functionaries** — city government staff (Enlace PQRSD, Asesor Jurídico). Work at a desk during office hours. They process high volumes of PQRSD, must spot SLA alerts instantly, validate AI classifications, and generate structured summaries. Their job: work fast and make defensible decisions.

### Brand Personality

Voice: **formal, trustworthy, clear** — Alcaldía de Medellín is a government body. Not warm-and-approachable, not bold-and-innovative. Institutional confidence: the interface should feel like a well-run office, not a startup or a generic web form.

Emotional goal for citizens: reassurance. "My request was received. I will be responded to within the law."
Emotional goal for functionaries: control. "I can see everything I need, act quickly, and nothing will slip past me."

### Aesthetic Direction

**Citizen portal** — Light theme. Medellín green (#00a859) used sparingly as a trust signal, not decoration. Typography that reads as authoritative without being cold. Clean page structure, generous whitespace, very few cards. No hero metrics. The visual language of a serious public institution that has modernized.

**Functionary dashboard** — Dark theme with high contrast. Deep charcoal/forest-dark backgrounds (derived from the existing #1a3a2a brand). Medellín green as a precise accent only: active states, SLA-ok indicators, primary actions. SLA danger = red, no ambiguity. The aesthetic should feel like a real ops tool, not an admin panel template. NOT the AI dark-mode tell: no cyan glow, no purple gradients, no neon.

**Fonts**: The citizen portal needs something that reads like a government publication — authoritative but legible. The dashboard needs a font that survives dense data without fatigue. Both must be distinct from Bootstrap defaults.

**Theme split**: Two visually distinct surfaces — the public portal and the internal tool — so judges immediately understand there are two different products for two different audiences.

### Design Principles

1. **Clarity over flourish** — every element must earn its place. The goal is a 3-minute demo that needs no explanation.
2. **Institutional gravity** — the UI should signal that this is a legitimate government system, not a hackathon prototype. Typography and color restraint achieve this.
3. **Two-speed design** — citizen portal is slow and guided (one action at a time); functionary dashboard is dense and scannable (everything visible at a glance).
4. **Green as a rare accent** — Medellín green appears only where it means something: primary actions, confirmed states, safe SLA. Overuse kills its signaling value.
5. **Dark dashboard ≠ AI dark mode** — the functionary tool goes dark for readability and ops-tool feel, not for visual drama. No glow, no gradients, no cyan.
